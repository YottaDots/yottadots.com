# Pattern 2: Objection round on goals

![Visual summary of pattern 2, in four steps from left to right. 1. The centre proposes goals: draft OKRs and KPIs with the reasons behind them. 2. The centre answers every objection in writing: it accepts it, changes the goal, or explains why not. 4. The centre decides and publishes the goals together with the objection log. Below: the centre still decides, but not alone, and every answer is visible.](/img/diagrams/pattern-objection-round.svg)

<small>Status: **proposed (v0.1).** Supports [rule 2](../method/index.md#what-makes-it-a-federation).</small>

## In short

Before goals are final, value streams get a fixed period to object. The
centre must answer every objection in writing, and publishes the answers with
the goals. The centre still decides, but not alone.

## Context

Any planning cycle where the centre sets OKRs, KPIs or other targets that
value streams must reach: yearly, half-yearly or quarterly.

## Problem

Goals set at the top often miss what the people doing the work know: a target
that cannot be reached with the current capacity, two goals that conflict, or
a KPI that rewards the wrong behavior. Asking for "input" does not solve
this. Input can be collected and ignored, and everyone knows it. People then
stop giving it.

## Pattern

1. **The centre proposes.** Draft goals go to all value streams, with the
   reason behind each goal.
2. **Value streams review within a fixed window.** For example two weeks. They
   can raise **objections**: a reasoned case that a goal would cause harm,
   conflicts with another goal, or cannot be reached. A preference ("we would
   rather do something else") is not an objection.
3. **The centre answers every objection in writing.** There are three possible
   answers:
   - **accept:** the goal changes as proposed;
   - **amend:** the goal changes in another way that meets the concern;
   - **explain:** the goal stays, with the reason why.
4. **The centre decides and publishes** the final goals together with the
   objection log: each objection and its answer.

The window, the format and the cycle are up to each organization. What is
fixed: objections are possible, each one is answered, and the answers are
public.

## Why it works

The obligation to answer is what makes the difference. A centre that must
explain in public why it ignores a reasoned objection will think twice before
ignoring it. And value streams that see their objections answered, even when
the answer is no, have a reason to keep raising them.

The idea is borrowed from consent decision-making in sociocracy and from
Holacracy's integrative decision-making, where a valid objection must be dealt
with before a proposal passes. YottaDots applies it to goals, but keeps the
final decision with the centre. That keeps it workable for one organization
with one board.

## Guardrails

- An objection must give a **reason**. Objections without a reason do not
  need an answer.
- **Every** reasoned objection gets an answer. No silent rejections.
- The objection log is visible to **all** value streams, not only the one that
  objected.
- Raising an objection must be **safe**. It may never count against a value
  stream or its lead.

## Signals

- Objections are raised every cycle. Zero objections over several cycles
  usually means people have given up, not that goals are perfect.
- Some objections lead to changes. If none ever do, the round is theatre.
- Value streams say the final goals are reachable, even when they did not
  get everything they asked for.

## Anti-patterns

- **Input theatre.** A survey or town hall instead of objections that must be
  answered.
- **Endless rounds.** Objections reopen the debate again and again. The
  centre answers once, then decides.
- **Veto by the back door.** Value streams treat objections as a veto. Under
  YottaDots they are not: the centre decides.
- **Late goals.** The window is skipped because "we are already late".

## Evidence

- Consent decision-making is used in sociocratic organizations and in
  Holacracy. There it applies to governance decisions, not to company goals.
- **[Needs Evidence]** That an objection round on goals leads to better or
  more widely supported goals than ordinary top-down OKR setting. No YottaDots
  cases yet.
