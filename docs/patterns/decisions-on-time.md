# Pattern 11: Decisions on time

Below: a decision that waits has a cost; make the wait visible.](/img/diagrams/pattern-decisions-on-time.svg)

## In short

Every open decision of the centre gets one owner and a **"decide by"** date.
Open, overdue and decided decisions are visible to everyone. A simple
**decision health check**, with three measures, shows whether the centre is
deciding on time.

## Context

Any organization where value streams depend on decisions of the centre:
priorities, budgets, new teams, guardrails, answers to proposals and
objections.

## Problem

Decisions that are not made, or made too late, block everything else. Teams
wait, work in parallel on options that will be dropped, or decide themselves
and are corrected later. The cost of waiting is invisible: it shows up as
delays and frustration elsewhere, never as "a decision we did not take". And
the higher the decision sits, the harder it is for anyone below to ask when it
will come.

## Pattern

1. **Log every open decision.** What needs deciding, who asked, who in the
   centre owns it (one person or role), and what is needed to decide.
2. **Set a "decide by" date** when the question arrives, together with the
   person who asked. As a default, use the same answer time as for proposals:
   within one planning cycle or 30 days, whichever is shorter. Urgent
   decisions get an earlier date.
3. **Make it visible.** A decision board that every value stream can see,
   with four columns: *open*, *due soon*, *overdue* and *decided*. Decided
   items show the decision and the reason.
4. **Decide, or move the date in the open.** If more time is really needed,
   the owner sets a new date and says why. Postponing is allowed; postponing
   in silence is not.
5. **Run a decision health check** every planning cycle, with three measures:
   - **lead time:** how long from question to decision;
   - **on time:** the share of decisions made by their date;
   - **backlog:** how many decisions are open, and how many are overdue.

   Add one question to the value streams: *which decision are you waiting
   for most?*
6. **Act on the patterns.** Decisions that are often late may belong at a
   lower level: take them off the [central list](./name-what-is-central.md).
   Or the centre may need to make fewer decisions, or prepare them better.

## Why it works

A named owner and a visible date turn a vague delay into a commitment that
anyone can check. Overdue decisions become visible to the people who wait for
them, so the cost of waiting is no longer hidden. And the health check gives
the centre facts about its own decision making, which helps it decide what to
keep on the central list and what to let go.

The three measures follow the way Bain describes decision effectiveness
(quality, speed, how well decisions are carried out, and effort). The pattern
measures speed directly; the reasons on the board and the question to the
value streams give a first view of the rest.

## Guardrails

- **One owner per decision,** not a committee.
- **Confidential decisions,** for example about people or acquisitions, may
  show only that a decision is open and its date, not its content.
- **No blame for an honest postponement** with a reason. The point is
  visibility, not punishment.
- **Speed is not the only goal.** A quick bad decision is not better than a
  slightly later good one.

## Signals

- Value streams know when the decisions they wait for will come.
- The share of decisions made on time goes up, and lead times go down.
- Few decisions are postponed more than once.
- Decisions that keep being late move to a lower level.

## Anti-patterns

- **Date theatre.** Dates are moved quietly, so nothing is ever overdue.
- **Deciding "no" to beat the clock.** Saying no to everything makes the
  numbers look good. Watch the reasons.
- **A board nobody looks at.** Visible means discussed, for example at the
  start of every centre meeting.
- **Logging everything.** Only decisions that someone else is waiting for
  belong on the board.

## Evidence

- In a McKinsey survey, only about one in five respondents said their
  organization was good at decision making, and the better organizations made
  decisions at the right level and quickly. Bain reports that companies with
  high decision effectiveness also perform better. Both are consultancy
  surveys in which people judge their own organization.
- **[Needs Evidence]** That visible deadlines and a decision health check make
  a centre decide faster without lowering the quality of its decisions. No
  YottaDots cases yet. The 30-day default is a starting point.
