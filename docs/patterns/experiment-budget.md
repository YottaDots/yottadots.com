# Pattern 10: Experiment budget

## In short

Each value stream may use a small, fixed share of its budget or capacity,
for example up to 5 to 10 percent, to test ideas without asking for
approval. Experiments are short, reversible and within the guardrails.
Results are shared, also when an idea fails. A successful experiment becomes
a proposal to the centre, with the evidence.

## Context

Organizations where good ideas from the people doing the work die before
anyone has tried them, because they need approval first and the approver
wants proof.

## Problem

New ideas are caught in a loop: they need approval to be tried, and they need
to be tried to be approved. The [proposal route](./proposal-route.md) makes
sure ideas are heard, but the centre still has to judge them on paper. Many
ideas that would work never get the chance, and the ones that do are often
the ones the boss already liked.

## Pattern

1. **Set the budget once a year.** The centre sets the share for each value
   stream, for example up to 5 to 10 percent of its budget or capacity.
2. **Experiment without approval,** within these limits:
   - a stated **hypothesis**: what we expect, and how we will know;
   - **time-boxed**: at most about three months;
   - **reversible**: it can be stopped without lasting damage;
   - **within all guardrails**, for example on security, privacy and law.
3. **Do not use it for lasting commitments:** no permanent teams, no long
   contracts, nothing that leaves years of maintenance. Those are decisions
   about the what (see [Adding capacity is a choice too](./adding-capacity-is-a-choice.md)).
4. **Share the results,** also when the idea failed.
5. **Scale up through a proposal.** A successful experiment goes to the centre
   as a [proposal](./proposal-route.md), with the evidence.

## Why it works

Evidence beats opinion. An idea that has been tried, even on a small scale,
is much harder to dismiss than an idea on paper, whoever came up with it. A
fixed, limited budget keeps the risk small and predictable for the centre,
and removes the need to ask permission for every small test. Sharing failures
means the organization learns from all experiments, not only from the
successes.

## Guardrails

- The budget is **small and fixed.** It is not a second budget to work around
  the centre's priorities.
- Experiments stay **within all guardrails.**
- **No lasting commitments** without a decision of the centre.
- In the public sector, use the room the budget rules allow, and say so where
  there is none.

## Signals

- Value streams run experiments every year, and share the results.
- Some proposals to the centre come with experiment results.
- Some experiments fail, and that is reported without blame.

## Anti-patterns

- **Hidden project.** An "experiment" grows into a permanent team or system
  without a decision.
- **Never used.** The budget exists, but nobody dares to spend it.
- **Only successes reported.** Failures are hidden, so the lessons are lost.
- **Budget raid.** The centre takes the experiment budget back whenever money
  is tight.

## Evidence

- Setting aside time or money for self-chosen work is known from companies
  such as 3M and Google, though how well it works is debated.
- **[Needs Evidence]** That a fixed experiment budget per value stream leads
  to more and better ideas being adopted, without creating sprawl. No
  YottaDots cases yet. The 5 to 10 percent and three months are starting
  points.
