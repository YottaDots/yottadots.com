## In short

Value streams can put their own ideas for the what to the centre: a new goal,
a shift in priorities, a new product or market, or stopping something. The
centre must answer every proposal in writing, within one planning cycle or 30
days, whichever is shorter. Proposals and answers are visible to everyone.
The centre still decides.

## Context

Organizations where the centre sets the goals, and where the people closest
to customers and the work often see opportunities and problems first.

## Problem

In a hierarchy, a new idea has to get past the boss. It may stall because the
manager did not think of it, because it would weaken the manager's position,
or simply because nobody has to respond. The objection round
([pattern 2](./objection-round-on-goals.md)) gives value streams a voice, but
only in reaction to what the centre proposes. Ideas that start in a value
stream have no route.

## Pattern

1. **Write it on one page.** What is proposed, why, the expected effect, and
   what it would replace or stop.
2. **Collect proposals for the planning round.** Proposals go into the next
   regular goal-setting round, so the centre handles them together with its own
   priorities. Urgent proposals can be handled earlier.
3. **Answer in writing, on time.** Within one planning cycle or 30 days,
   whichever is shorter, the centre answers each proposal:
   - **accept:** the proposal becomes part of the goals;
   - **amend:** a changed version is accepted;
   - **decline:** with the reasons.
4. **Publish proposals and answers** for all value streams, in the same log as
   the objections.
5. **Test before proposing, where useful.** A value stream can first test an
   idea with its [experiment budget](./experiment-budget.md) and bring the
   results with the proposal.

## Why it works

The obligation to answer, in public and on time, is what makes the
difference. An idea that must be answered cannot be ignored or quietly
buried. A centre that declines must give reasons that others can read, which
makes "not invented here" or self-interest harder to hide. And because the
centre still decides, the route does not create a second centre of power.

## Guardrails

- The centre still decides. A proposal is not a vote and not a veto.
- One page per proposal. Long business cases are not required to be heard.
- Making a proposal may never count against a value stream or its lead.
- A declined proposal can come back with new evidence, for example from an
  experiment.

## Signals

- Proposals arrive every cycle, from several value streams.
- Some proposals become goals.
- Answers are on time, and the reasons for declining are specific.

## Anti-patterns

- **Black hole.** Proposals are collected but never answered.
- **Polite no.** Every proposal is declined with the same general reason.
- **Proposal flood.** Value streams use proposals to lobby for budget. Batching
  into the planning round and the one-page format help.
- **Back channel.** The real decisions are still made in informal talks with
  the boss.

## Evidence

- Proposals followed by objections are standard in sociocratic and Holacracy
  governance meetings, for governance decisions.
- **[Needs Evidence]** That a written, time-bound right to propose on company
  goals leads to more and better ideas being adopted. No YottaDots cases yet.
