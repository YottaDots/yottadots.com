## What it is

The Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) is a framework for running agile work at the
scale of a whole portfolio or enterprise. It was created by Dean Leffingwell
and is maintained and trained by Scaled Agile, Inc. It is one of the most
widely used scaling frameworks.

## Core idea

Organize around value streams. Teams of teams, called Agile Release Trains,
plan and deliver together on a fixed rhythm (the Program Increment, with PI
Planning). Above them, Lean Portfolio Management connects strategy to funding.

## The seven questions

| # | Question | SAFe |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who decides the what? | Lean Portfolio Management: strategic themes, portfolio vision, value stream budgets |
| 2 | Do units help shape goals? | Partly: participatory budgeting involves business owners and other stakeholders in allocating budgets to value streams |
| 3 | What happens to unnamed decisions? | Not described (as far as we found) |
| 4 | Can units opt out of shared services? | Not described (as far as we found) |
| 5 | Who decides the how? | Partly the teams, within prescribed roles and events (release trains, PI Planning) |
| 6 | Practical guidance | Very detailed: roles, events, artifacts, training and certification |
| 7 | Track record | Widely used; there are also documented cases of organizations moving away from it |

## Strengths

- **Complete and detailed.** Few frameworks describe as much, from team to
  portfolio.
- **Funding by value stream** instead of by project, with *Lean budget
  guardrails* for spending and governance.
- **Participatory budgeting:** a worked-out way to let the people closest to
  the work help allocate money.

## Common criticisms

- **Too heavy and hierarchical.** Ken Schwaber (co-creator of Scrum) and
  David J. Anderson (Kanban) criticized SAFe publicly in 2013. Scaled Agile
  responded, and others pointed out that the critics had not practiced SAFe
  themselves.
- **Structures that outlive their use.** Austrian Post adopted SAFe and later
  replaced it with a minimal set of its own rules, reporting problems such as
  meeting load and a pace that could not be sustained at train level.

## When it fits, and when it does not

- **Fits:** large organizations with many dependent teams working on the same
  products, which want a complete, ready-made model with training.
- **Fits less:** organizations whose units work largely independently, or that
  want to keep their method free.

## How it relates to YottaDots

| | |
|---|---|
| **Compete** | At portfolio level. Both describe how the centre steers and funds. SAFe has no opt-out or residual-power rule. |
| **Combine** | At value stream level. A value stream can choose to run as a SAFe-style release train as its *how*. |
| **Learn** | Participatory budgeting is a candidate practice for YottaDots rule 2 (value streams help shape the what). |
