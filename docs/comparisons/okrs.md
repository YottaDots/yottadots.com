## What it is

Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) is a goal-setting method. Andy Grove
introduced it at Intel in the 1970s; John Doerr brought it to Google and
described it in *Measure What Matters* (2018). It is not an organization
model, but it is often the way the centre expresses its goals.

## Core idea

An **objective** says what you want to achieve; two to five **key results**
make it measurable. Leadership sets company objectives; teams write their own
objectives and key results that contribute to them.

## The seven questions

| # | Question | OKRs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who decides the what? | Leadership sets company objectives |
| 2 | Do units help shape goals? | Partly: Doerr recommends that teams and individuals write roughly half of their own OKRs, in consultation with their managers |
| 3 | What happens to unnamed decisions? | Not described (as far as we found) |
| 4 | Can units opt out of shared services? | Not in scope |
| 5 | Who decides the how? | The teams. OKRs do not prescribe a method |
| 6 | Practical guidance | Moderate: widely documented and simple to start |
| 7 | Track record | Long: Intel since the 1970s, Google and many others |

## Strengths

- **Simple.** One page of objectives and key results is enough to start.
- **Top-down and bottom-up at once,** if done as intended.
- **Widely known,** with a long track record.

## Common criticisms

- **Becomes a top-down cascade** or a checklist of activities instead of
  outcomes.
- **Prerequisites are often missing.** Marty Cagan, long an advocate, says he
  has stopped recommending OKRs for most companies he meets, because they lack
  empowered teams and team (not individual) objectives.

## When it fits, and when it does not

- **Fits:** almost any organization that wants to make goals explicit and
  measurable.
- **Fits less:** if leaders use them to assign activities, or link them
  directly to individual bonuses.

## How it relates to YottaDots

| | |
|---|---|
| **Compete** | No. OKRs are a goal method, not an organization model. |
| **Combine** | The centre can use OKRs to express the *what*; value streams write their own contributing OKRs. |
| **Learn** | Teams writing a large share of their own key results is a simple, proven form of YottaDots rule 2. |
