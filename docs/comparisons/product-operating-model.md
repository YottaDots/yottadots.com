## What it is

The Product Operating Model is the way of working described by Marty Cagan and
Silicon Valley Product Group (SVPG), in the books *Inspired*, *Empowered*
(with Chris Jones) and *Transformed* (2024). It describes how strong product
companies decide what to build and how teams work.

## Core idea

Give cross-functional product teams **problems to solve, not features to
build**, and hold them accountable for outcomes. Product leaders provide the
product vision and strategy; teams find the best solution through product
discovery.

## The seven questions

| # | Question | Product Operating Model |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who decides the what? | Product leaders: product vision, product strategy, team topology, and which team solves which problem |
| 2 | Do units help shape goals? | Partly: team objectives result from a negotiation between leaders and teams |
| 3 | What happens to unnamed decisions? | Not described (as far as we found) |
| 4 | Can units opt out of shared services? | Not described. Team topology, including platform teams, is decided by leadership |
| 5 | Who decides the how? | Partly the teams: they choose the solution, within the empowered product team model |
| 6 | Practical guidance | Detailed: books, articles and coaching |
| 7 | Track record | Grown from tech product companies; commentators note that few companies work this way yet [Needs Evidence: independent studies] |

## Strengths

- **Outcomes over output.** Teams are judged on results, not on delivering a
  feature list.
- **A clear role for leaders:** set the vision and strategy, give context, and
  coach. Not: hand out solutions.
- **Deep, practical guidance** on product discovery and product leadership.

## Common criticisms

- **Demanding.** It needs strong product leaders and skilled teams. Without
  them, "empowered" stays a word.
- **Mainly from tech product companies.** Applying it to organizations that do
  not build digital products takes translation.

## When it fits, and when it does not

- **Fits:** organizations that build and run digital products and want teams to
  own outcomes.
- **Fits less:** work that is mainly operations, projects with fixed scope, or
  organizations without product leadership.

## How it relates to YottaDots

| | |
|---|---|
| **Compete** | Little. It describes how product work is done; YottaDots describes who decides what. |
| **Combine** | A value stream can run as one or more empowered product teams as its *how*. |
| **Learn** | "Problems to solve, not features to build" is a sharp way to express *the centre decides the what*. |
