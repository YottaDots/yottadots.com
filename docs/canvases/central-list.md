# The Central List Canvas

A worksheet for the first step of YottaDots: agree, in writing, **what the
centre decides**. Everything that is not on the list stays with the value
streams.

[![The Central List Canvas. Section 1, What the centre decides: a table with the columns decision, why central, owner and review date, with rows for mission and vision, strategy, ranked goals, agreed guardrails, capacity and money, and other decisions that need a strong reason. Section 2, Stays with the value streams: examples such as method, planning, team set-up, tools, roles, meetings and priorities within the goals, plus space for more. Section 3, Grey zone: decisions where it is unclear who decides; until the list changes, the value stream decides. Section 4, How this list changes: with fields for the owner of the list, where it is published and the next review. ## When to use it

- **At the start,** as step 1 of the
  [first route](../method/big-picture.md#bottom-a-possible-first-route).
- **When people often ask "who decides this?"** or need permission for
  things nobody ever gave the centre.
- **Once a year,** to review the list and remove what the centre no longer
  needs to decide.

## Who takes part

The centre (for example the board or management team) and representatives of
the value streams. The centre owns the list; the value streams make sure it
is complete and honest.

## How to fill it in

A suggested workshop of about 90 minutes. Adapt it to your context.

| Step | Section | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1. What the centre decides** | The centre lists the decisions it takes today, row by row. For each one: *why* it must be central, *who* owns it, and *when* it will be reviewed. | 30 min |
| 2 | **1. (challenge)** | The value streams challenge each line: is this a decision, or a department? Is the reason good enough? Could the value streams decide it? | 20 min |
| 3 | **2. Stays with the value streams** | Make explicit what people often ask permission for today, such as tools, team set-up or meetings. | 10 min |
| 4 | **3. Grey zone** | Write down every decision where nobody is sure who decides. Give each one a date by which it will be resolved. Until then, the value stream decides. | 15 min |
| 5 | **4. How this list changes** | Name the owner of the list, where it will be published, and the next review date. | 5 min |
| 6 | **5. Check** | Tick the five boxes. If one fails, fix it before you publish. | 10 min |

## The five checks

1. **Fits on one page.** A long list means the centre decides too much.
2. **Names decisions, not departments.** "IT" is not a decision; "the approved
   cloud providers" is.
3. **No catch-all item.** "And other matters of strategic importance" turns the
   list into a blank cheque.
4. **Public to everyone,** not only to managers.
5. **Includes the grey-zone rule:** when in doubt, the value stream decides.

## Tips

- **Start from real decisions,** not from an organization chart. Ask: what did
  the centre decide in the last three months?
- **Capacity and money** have their own row on purpose: a new team, supplier
  or budget is a decision about the what (see
  [Adding capacity is a choice too](../patterns/adding-capacity-is-a-choice.md)).
- **Guardrails** on the list should each pass the
  [guardrail test](../patterns/guardrails-that-earn-their-place.md).
- **Changing the list later** is a decision of its own. Value streams can
  object ([pattern 2](../patterns/objection-round-on-goals.md)) or propose
  ([pattern 9](../patterns/proposal-route.md)).
