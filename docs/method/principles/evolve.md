# Principle 4: Evolve

![Visual summary of the Evolve principle, a loop of four steps. 1. Design: treat every choice as a hypothesis. 2. Try it in practice, small first. 3. Look at the signals: what works and what does not. 4. Review: keep, change or stop. Then back to design. Examples of what goes through the loop: the list of central decisions, the guardrails, the patterns and the way of working of each value stream. Below: every design choice is a hypothesis.](/img/diagrams/principle-evolve.svg)

<small>Status: principle of v0.1. Explanation **proposed**.</small>

## In short

Treat the organization as a system that keeps learning. Every design choice
is a hypothesis. Try it, look at what happens, and keep, change or stop it.

## What it means

No organizational design is right forever. Markets, people, technology and
laws change, and a design that worked last year can get in the way next
year. Evolve means:

- **Design choices are hypotheses.** The list of central decisions, the
  guardrails, the value streams and the way of working are all choices that
  may turn out to be wrong.
- **Changes are small and frequent,** rather than large and rare. A big
  reorganization every few years is replaced by regular, smaller adjustments.
- **Reviews are built in.** The design includes moments to check whether it
  still works, with a real option to stop things.
- **Stopping is part of change.** When something new is introduced, decide
  what it replaces, and stop that.

## Why it matters

- **Rules pile up.** Without regular review, rules, meetings and reports
  stay long after their reason has gone.
- **Big reorganizations are costly.** They disrupt work for months and often
  layer the new structure on top of the old one.
- **The method itself can be wrong.** YottaDots is young. Its rules and
  patterns also need to be tested and improved.

## In practice

- Guardrails are reviewed regularly and removed when they cannot justify
  themselves:
  [Guardrails that earn their place](../../patterns/guardrails-that-earn-their-place.md).
- The list of central decisions is reviewed every year:
  [Name what is central](../../patterns/name-what-is-central.md).
- What was built is checked for use, and removed when nobody uses it:
  [Usage check](../../patterns/usage-check.md).
- Patterns start as **proposed** and become **accepted** only when real cases
  show that they work ([Patterns](../../patterns/index.md#evidence)).
- Value streams improve their own way of working, as part of the free how.

## What it does not mean

- **Not constant reorganization.** Evolving is about small, deliberate
  adjustments, not change for its own sake.
- **Not "anything goes".** The four rules stay in place while their
  application improves.
- **Not only the centre's job.** Value streams evolve their how; the centre
  and value streams together evolve the what and the guardrails.

## Questions to ask yourself

- When did you last stop a rule, meeting or report?
- When you introduced something new, what did you stop?
- Which of your design choices would you like to test?

## Evidence

- Continuous improvement and learning organizations are well-established
  ideas in management practice.
- **[Needs Evidence]** That built-in reviews (of guardrails and of the central
  list) keep a YottaDots organization lean over time. No cases yet.
