# Principle 1: Federate

The value streams decide the how: method, planning, team set-up, tools, and everything that is not named as central. An arrow shows that anything not on the centre's list goes to the value streams. Below: decide where the knowledge is; share only what must be shared.](/img/diagrams/principle-federate.svg)

<small>Status: principle of v0.1. Explanation **proposed**.</small>

## In short

The centre decides **what** the organization wants to achieve. Value streams
decide **how** they achieve it. Everything not named as central stays with
the value streams.

## What it means

A YottaDots organization has two levels of decision-making:

- **The centre** (a board, management team or holding) decides the what:
  mission, vision, strategy, OKRs, KPIs and the guardrails that apply to
  everyone.
- **Value streams** decide the how: which method they use, how they plan,
  how they set up their teams, which tools they use, and anything else that
  has not been named as central.

The centre must name what it decides. What it does not name, it does not
decide. This is what turns "teams get some freedom" into a federation: the
freedom is the default, and every central power has to be made explicit.

## Why it matters

- **Knowledge sits where the work is.** The people who serve a customer,
  run a process or build a product usually know best how to do it. Decisions
  taken far from that knowledge are slower and often worse.
- **Some things must be shared.** One organization needs one direction, one
  set of legal obligations and one balance sheet. Leaving everything to the
  value streams leads to fragmentation.
- **Central power grows unless it is limited.** Without a clear line, the
  centre takes over decisions one reasonable request at a time. Naming what
  is central stops that drift.

The idea is old. It is the principle of **subsidiarity**: decide at the
lowest level that can decide well, and pass upwards only what that level
cannot handle.

## In practice

- The centre publishes a short list of what it decides:
  [Name what is central](../../patterns/name-what-is-central.md).
- Value streams may refuse shared services that are not agreed as a
  guardrail:
  [Opt-out of shared services](../../patterns/opt-out-of-shared-services.md).
- Value streams choose their own delivery method: Scrum, Kanban, projects,
  PRINCE2, campaigns or a mix.

## What it does not mean

- **Not "the centre does nothing".** The centre sets direction and is
  accountable for it. Federate limits the centre; it does not remove it.
- **Not "every team for itself".** Value streams are bound by the shared
  goals and the agreed guardrails.
- **Not a fixed structure.** YottaDots does not prescribe how many value
  streams there are or how they are cut.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Can anyone in the organization find out what the centre decides?
- Can a value stream change its way of working without asking permission?
- When it is unclear who decides, who wins?

## Evidence

- Subsidiarity is a long-standing principle in political and organizational
  thought, and many federal constitutions leave unnamed powers with the
  member states.
- **[Needs Evidence]** That federating inside one organization leads to
  faster or better decisions than the alternatives. Research on
  decentralization shows mixed results that depend on context.
