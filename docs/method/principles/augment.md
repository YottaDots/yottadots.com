# Principle 3: Augment

![Visual summary of the Augment principle, before and after. Before: information travels up and down through layers of people, from a team to a manager to another manager and back, and much of the work is routing, reporting and checking. After: AI co-workers take on the routing, summaries, drafts and checks. People spend their time on judgment and decisions, and a named human owns every result. Below: AI takes on what needs no human judgment.](/img/diagrams/principle-augment.svg)

<small>Status: principle of v0.1. Explanation **proposed**. Open question: how
the idea of AI as a "co-worker" fits with human accountability.</small>

## In short

Use AI to strengthen what people can do, and to reduce the cost of
coordinating work. AI takes on what needs no human judgment. A named human
owns every result.

## What it means

Augment has two parts:

- **Strengthen people.** AI drafts, analyses, checks and summarises, so that
  people can do more of the work that needs judgment, creativity and
  relationships.
- **Make coordination cheaper.** A large part of organizational work is
  coordination: reports, status meetings, hand-offs, keeping everyone
  informed. AI can do part of that work faster and at lower cost.

AI is a co-worker, not a decision-maker. It can do real work, but it cannot
be held accountable, so a human always owns the result.

## Why it matters

- **Coordination cost shapes organizations.** Layers of management exist
  partly to collect, filter and pass on information. If AI makes that work
  cheaper, an organization may need fewer layers and can give value streams
  more room. That makes federation more practical.
- **Adding AI is not enough.** Putting AI on top of every step of an old
  process keeps the old process. The bigger gain often comes from redesigning
  the work, and sometimes from dropping steps altogether (see
  [AI as a co-worker](../../ai-coworker/index.md)).
- **Unowned AI work is a risk.** When AI output passes through many hands,
  each person may assume someone else checked it.

## In practice

- Every AI task has a named human owner:
  [AI with a human owner](../../patterns/ai-with-a-human-owner.md).
- Value streams decide how they use AI. That is part of the free how, within
  agreed guardrails such as privacy and data.
- AI use is visible to the people affected. YottaDots itself works this way:
  [How we use AI](../../ai-coworker/how-we-use-ai.md).

## What it does not mean

- **Not "AI decides".** People decide and remain accountable.
- **Not replacing people as the goal.** The aim is better work and cheaper
  coordination, not fewer people for its own sake.
- **Not AI everywhere.** Work that needs human judgment, care or trust stays
  with people.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Which coordination work (reports, status updates, hand-offs) could AI take
  on?
- For each AI task, who is the owner?
- Did you add AI to a process, or redesign the process?

## Evidence

- Human oversight of AI is required in regulation and guidance, such as the
  EU AI Act for high-risk systems.
- **[Hypothesis]** Cheaper coordination through AI allows flatter,
  more federated organizations.
- **[Needs Evidence]** That AI reduces coordination overhead in practice,
  without new overhead for checking AI output.
